This is not yet a column. It is the space where a column will go, set in type so that the compositor may see how the type behaves before he is asked to say anything with it. A first paragraph carries the capital; the capital is cut from the headline face and dropped three lines deep, which is the only ornament this page permits itself.

<Aside>An apostille, set to the margin in italic and ruled in red, for whatever the paragraph beside it could not decently say aloud.</Aside>

The paragraphs that follow are set ragged, on a measure of forty ems, with a generous lead between the lines. The front page justifies its columns because a front page is a crowd; a column is a conversation, and a conversation is not justified. *Italic* is available for emphasis, **bold** for the rare occasion that warrants it, and a [link](/) for pointing the reader elsewhere.

## A subhead, to prove the second size

Subheads are cut in the headline face at a smaller size and given air above and below. They divide a column into rooms. A column of eight minutes wants three or four of them; a column of four wants none, and should be ashamed to ask.

<Figure src={plate} caption="A plate, set to prove the frame; it expands when pressed." credit="BLOCK No. 0001" />

Plates are set in a frame of sixteen by ten, screened to sit with the type, and captioned below with the block number to the right. A plate that has not yet arrived shows its frame empty, with a notice in the middle, so that the reader knows a picture was intended and the compositor knows he owes one.

> “A quotation is set between double rules, centred, in the headline face — the only time the column raises its voice.”

<Figure caption="A photograph, not yet arrived; the frame holds its place." credit="BLOCK No. 0002" kind="photograph" />

That is the whole of the vocabulary: capital, paragraph, apostille, subhead, plate, quotation, and the sign-off below the rule. Nothing else is permitted, on the grounds that anything the vocabulary cannot say is probably not worth setting.

<Aside>The vocabulary is small on purpose. A larger one would only tempt the writer into using it.</Aside>

When there is something to say, this entry will be lifted from the forme and a real one set in its place. Until then it stands as proof — of the type, not of the man.
